# 02-debate - WORKED EXAMPLE - fictional
<!-- file-class: DOCTRINE -->

*Worked example. The chair's synthesis, written after all five briefs landed.
Nothing here is smoothed: one disagreement is carried forward live, and the
pressure tests are left unanswered on the seats' behalf.*

---

## 1. Agreements

These converged without prompting. The five briefs were written in parallel and
none of their authors could see another, which is what makes agreement here worth
something.

**Nobody argues for declining.** Not one brief proposes walking away. Revenue,
Operations, Finance and General Counsel disagree about size, sequence and the
binding constraint, and all four assume some version of this account gets taken.
That is the strongest signal in the set: this meeting is about shape, not about
yes or no.

**Measure before committing.** Operations asks for it directly. Revenue will not
carry a figure Operations has not stood behind. General Counsel says the volume
blank waits for the measurement. Three seats, three motives, one sequencing rule.

**No number travels alone.** Finance wants the cash consequence attached to every
volume figure; standards-guard wants a source attached to every measured figure.
Same discipline, different failure mode, both cheap at drafting time and
expensive at the gate.

**A ramp without a named trigger is a promise, not a plan.** Operations and
General Counsel say this in different vocabularies, one operational and one
contractual, and neither had read the other.

## 2. Live disagreement, carried forward

**Revenue against Operations: counter for the full order on a phased ramp, or cap
the account at partial volume permanently?**

*Revenue's position, in its own words:* a permanent cap tells the buyer today
what our ceiling is, forever, and an opening counter below what we can hold reads
to a professional buyer as a supplier who is not ready.

*Operations' position, in its own words:* the position is not a negotiating
stance, it is arithmetic that has not been done yet, and a ramp whose second step
has no trigger leaves this seat holding the bag.

**This is not resolved here and the chair is not resolving it.** The two seats
disagree about a real thing, they hold different halves of the evidence, and the
half that would settle it, the measurement, does not exist yet. It goes into
`03-plan.md` as a rulings-table row, with the chair's recommendation attached and
argued so the operator can overrule it in one word.

A third position sits underneath both and belongs on the record: **Finance says
they are arguing about the wrong constraint.** Neither Revenue nor Operations
priced the sixty-day cycle. If Finance is right, the capacity answer can be
correct and the account still sink the business. Finance framed it as a
constraint rather than a plan, because it is an advisory function, but it is the
most consequential thing any brief said and being politely worded is no reason to
demote it to a footnote.

## 3. Pressure tests

The chair's own probing of the weakest point in each position. These are left
open. They are the questions the plan and the adversarial pass have to survive,
not questions the seats are being asked to answer here.

**To Revenue.** You say a counter below our real capacity reads as unready. What
does a missed delivery in month three read as? You named that risk yourself and
then recommended the option carrying more of it.

**To Operations.** Your estimate is low twenties against a rated thirty-four, and
you say plainly it is an estimate. If the measurement comes back at the top of
your range, does your position change, or was the caution about something other
than the number?

**To Finance.** Suppose he answers in cycles rather than currency, one and not
two. Is that enough for your constraint to bind, or does the plan then need a
figure it cannot get?

**To General Counsel.** You concede the header and the flags are conventions, not
mechanisms. If a hurried reader can treat a flagged draft as ready, what in this
plan makes the flag do work other than the operator noticing it?

**To all four.** Every brief assumes the offer stays on the table while we
measure. Nobody tested that and nobody in this room can. It is a fact about the
buyer, and it belongs to the operator.
