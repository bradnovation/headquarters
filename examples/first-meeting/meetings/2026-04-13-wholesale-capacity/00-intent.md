## The operator's intent, verbatim

> The grocery group came back and they want a standing order across all six
> stores. It's about double what we roast now. My gut says yes because that's
> the kind of account that makes the wholesale line real instead of a side
> thing, but I don't actually know if the roaster can do it and I don't love
> that they pay on sixty days. I don't want a plan that assumes I'll just buy a
> bigger roaster. Tell me whether to take it, take part of it, or walk.

## Constraints the intent already settles

The briefs argue how, not whether. These are not open questions:

1. **No new roasting equipment this cycle.** The operator named it. A plan whose
   answer is a capital purchase is not an answer to this intent.
2. **The retail storefront is not degraded to serve wholesale.** Unstated in the
   message and confirmed by the operator when the chair asked, which is why it
   appears here rather than as a rulings-table row.
3. **The existing wholesale accounts keep their delivery cadence.** Taking a new
   account by quietly slipping current ones is a decision, not a side effect, and
   the operator has not made it.

## Domain seats convened, and why

- **Revenue.** Owns the account relationship and the judgment on what this
  account is worth against the ones the roastery already has.
- **Operations.** Owns the registers, and owns the only question that can make
  every other answer moot: whether the roast hours exist. Convened here in a
  **dual role**, as a domain seat and as a standing advisory attendee, stated
  plainly because it is unusual.
- **General Counsel.** The function is convened as a domain participant, not
  merely as an advisory voice, because a standing order across six stores is a
  contract and the intent's substance is squarely terms and liability.

**Not convened:** Marketing and Engineering. Neither domain is touched. Over-
convening dilutes the debate and costs real money.

## Standing advisory attendees

standards-guard, Finance, and Operations attend by right. No justification is
owed for any of the three. Operations is also a convened domain seat here, noted
above.
