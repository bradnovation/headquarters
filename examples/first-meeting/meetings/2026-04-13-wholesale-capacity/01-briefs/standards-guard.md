*Worked example. Advisory brief: gates and constraints only, no plan of its own.
Written independently, without sight of any other brief.*

---

## 1. Position

Whatever this meeting rules, nothing it produces seals without passing here
first, and two properties of the likely deliverables decide whether that pass is
cheap or painful.

**Every capacity figure needs a named source.** A counter-offer that asserts a
weekly volume without pointing at the measurement it came from will fail the
provenance check, and it should. The fix costs one clause at drafting time and a
rewrite afterward.

**A term sheet is outward-facing by shape even while it sits in a drawer.** It
gets checked against the same bar as anything else the operator might one day
hand to a third party, and General Counsel gates it in addition, because money
and liability are plainly in play.

## 2. What this function needs

- Each deliverable's originating seat recorded, so the verdict row can name it.
- Source lines on every measured figure, written as the figure is written rather
  than reconstructed at gate time.

## 3. Risks

- **Against a clean pass:** the counter-offer is the likeliest place for an
  unsourced claim, because persuasive writing and sourced writing pull in
  opposite directions.
- **Against this function:** it fails closed. If the check errors, nothing seals,
  and that will occasionally stop work for a reason that turns out to be
  mechanical. That is the intended cost.

## 4. Rulings worth asking

| Type | Ask | Recommended default |
|---|---|---|
| `[judgment]` | Does the gate run once at seal, or on each deliverable as it lands? | **On each deliverable.** A single pass at the end batches every finding into the least convenient moment. |
