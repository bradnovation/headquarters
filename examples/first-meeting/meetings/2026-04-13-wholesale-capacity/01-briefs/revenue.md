# Revenue seat brief - WORKED EXAMPLE - fictional
<!-- file-class: DOCTRINE -->

*Worked example. Written independently, without sight of any other brief.*

---

## 1. Position

**Counter for the whole order, phased.** Say yes in principle today and negotiate
the ramp, because what is on offer is not an order, it is a floor. Every other
wholesale account this roastery holds is a single cafe that can churn in a week
on one bad delivery or one new manager. Six stores under one buyer, on a standing
schedule, is the first thing in this business that would make next quarter
predictable.

The trade-off I am not hiding: concentration. Land this and one buyer becomes the
largest single line in the wholesale book, and the day they renegotiate they do
it from a position we handed them. I still want it. A roastery that refuses every
account large enough to matter has chosen a ceiling and called it prudence.

## 2. What this seat needs

- The real capacity number before I put any volume in writing. I will not carry a
  figure into a negotiation that Operations has not stood behind.
- The operator's own read on whether he wants this buyer at all, given the
  concentration. That is his call and I am not going to infer it.

## 3. Risks, including to my own position

- **Against me:** commit to a ramp we cannot roast and we do not lose the order,
  we lose the account and the reference. A missed delivery to a six-store chain
  is discussed by every other buyer in the region within a month.
- **Against me:** phasing sounds cooperative and is easy to promise. The second
  step is where suppliers quietly fail, and I would be the one who promised it.
- **Against declining:** the offer does not come back on the same terms.

## 4. Rulings worth asking

| Type | Ask | Recommended default |
|---|---|---|
| `[judgment]` | Counter for the full order on a phased ramp, or cap the account at partial volume permanently? | **Phased ramp.** A permanent cap tells the buyer today what our ceiling is, forever. |
| `[fact]` | Does the operator want this buyer at all, given that they become the largest single line in the book? | Safe provisional: draft the counter, prepare nothing that commits, and put no volume in front of anyone until he answers. |
