## F-1 - The measurement settles the meeting's live disagreement

**Filed 2026-04-20. Source: D1, one week of roast hours logged by shift.**

| | Hours per week |
|---|---|
| Machine rated | 34.0 |
| Measured usable | 22.5 |
| Of which estimated rather than counted | 1.5 |
| Already committed: storefront production | 9.0 |
| Already committed: existing wholesale accounts | 6.5 |
| **Free** | **7.0** |
| The grocery group's full standing order | 13.0 |

The full order needs 13 hours against 7 free. It does not fit, and no sequencing
of the week makes it fit. At half the standing order it needs 6.5 hours and fits
inside 7 with half an hour of slack, which is thin and is stated as thin rather
than rounded into comfort.

**This is where the Revenue-versus-Operations disagreement carried live out of
`02-debate.md` gets settled, and it is settled by measurement rather than by
argument.** Operations was right on the substance: the gap between rated and
usable hours was 11.5, a third of the machine, and nobody had ever counted it.
Revenue was right that the account was worth countering for rather than
declining, which is what the mission is producing. Neither seat was overruled by
the chair, and neither needed to be.

The 1.5 estimated hours are flagged in D1 itself: one shift's log was incomplete
and the number was reconstructed. That flag is why the ceiling sits at half the
order rather than at the arithmetic maximum the free hours would allow.

## F-2 - The cash ceiling and the roast ceiling land in the same place, independently

**Filed 2026-04-20. Source: D2, built to the one-cycle ceiling the operator
ruled.**

Half the standing order sits inside one sixty-day cycle of working capital. Any
step beyond it does not, because the green coffee for the larger volume is bought
and paid for before the first payment on the smaller volume arrives, which is a
second cycle of float by definition, and the operator ruled that he can carry one
and not two.

Two unrelated constraints, measured separately from different evidence, produced
the same ceiling. That is the most useful thing in this mission: it means the
counter-offer's number is not a negotiating position a determined buyer can push,
but the point where two independent walls meet.

**Operator dependency, posted to `registers/BLOCKED_ON_OPERATOR.md` the same
day.** The ramp's step two now needs a named trigger the operator has to accept
or amend, because it commits him to a future volume: step two opens only when
measured free hours have exceeded 12.0 for four consecutive weeks **and** the
buyer has paid two consecutive invoices within terms. The second condition is a
direct reading of his own ruling that he will not hire against an account that
has not proved it pays on time.

## F-3 - The payment terms are worth more than the volume negotiation

**Filed 2026-04-20. Source: D2, the thirty-day variant.**

Under thirty-day terms the cash ceiling stops binding first and the roast hours
bind alone, which moves the ceiling from half the order up to roughly the
free-hours maximum. The operator reserved the payment-terms conversation to
himself in `04-ruling.md` row 9, and the model gives him the reason it is worth
having: a term he can ask for in one sentence is worth more than anything this
staff can win arguing about volume.

The model recommends neither variant, exactly as the safe provisional required.
It states what each implies and stops.

## F-4 - What the two drafts carry

**Filed 2026-04-22. Source: D3 and D4.**

**D3, the counter-offer.** Half the standing order, ramp and trigger stated.
Every volume figure carries its source (F-1) and its cash consequence (F-2) in
the same sentence rather than in a separate section. One sentence was rewritten
at the standards gate for asserting a capacity claim without its source; the
rewrite is recorded in `STATE.md`. The draft sits in the operator's hands, and
nothing in this system can send it.

**D4, the term sheet skeleton.** Three flagged blanks and one flagged note:

- **Volume ceiling** - filled from F-1.
- **Governing law** - unfilled, and flagged as requiring selection with a
  qualified professional. No jurisdiction is assumed anywhere in the document.
- **Ramp trigger measurement source** - unfilled pending the operator's answer on
  the F-2 dependency.
- **Exclusivity** - a flagged note recording that exclusivity was ruled out
  entirely, rather than the silence the plan's default would have produced. The
  word does not appear in the document.

The document carries the standing header saying it is a draft prepared for the
operator's review and is not professional advice, and the recommend-human-review
flag rides with it. General Counsel passed it as a reviewed draft, which is not
an approval and is not advice.

## F-5 - What this mission did not answer

The offer's shelf life. Every brief assumed the offer stayed on the table for the
week the measurement took; nobody in the meeting could test that assumption and
this mission could not either. It is a fact about the buyer and it belongs to the
operator. Recorded here rather than dropped, so a successor session can tell a
question that was answered from one that was never anybody's to answer.
